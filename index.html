      📘 Research & Methodology

      Master's Project by Hamirul bin Othman (2026), Malaysia-Japan International Institute of Technology (MJIIT), Universiti Teknologi Malaysia.

      This study looks at how a gamified leaderboard can reduce anxiety and build autonomous motivation in a mathematically demanding chemical engineering course.

      🧭

      Autonomy

      Learners pick their own pseudonym and avatar, and choose how much social comparison they see.

      📈

      Competence

      Career tiers, progress bars and mastery badges make growth visible.

      🤝

      Relatedness

      The neighbour view and peer mentorship XP build a supportive cohort.

      Theoretical Grounding

      • Self-Determination Theory (Deci & Ryan): meeting the three basic psychological needs supports intrinsic motivation.
      • Goal-Setting Theory (Locke & Latham): specific, nearby goals (the next rank or neighbour) work better than distant ones.
      • RQ1, Anonymity & Social Comparison: pseudonyms and relative views guard against name-and-shame effects and stereotype threat.

      Methodology

      • Design: Convergent Parallel Mixed-Method (quantitative logs plus qualitative interviews, analysed in parallel and then merged).
      • Cohort: N = 25 SMJC 3263 undergraduates (Session 2026/2027-1).
      • Interview sampling: Maximal Variation Sampling (n = 6), with 2 participants each from the High, Moderate and At-risk engagement tiers.

      Synopsis

      This course introduces principles in separation processes between liquid-liquid, gas-liquid, vapour-liquid and solid-liquid separation processes. Unit operations covered include humidification, absorption, distillation, liquid-liquid extraction and solid-liquid extraction (leaching), applied through mass transfer principles.
